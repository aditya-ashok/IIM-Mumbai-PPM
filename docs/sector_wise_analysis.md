## Key Observations by Sector

### Finance — The Regulatory Powerhouse
The finance sector alone accounts for ~25% of all policies classified. Of these, **regulatory actions dominate** — RBI directions on defaulters, HFCs, P2P platforms; SEBI frameworks on market abuse, FPIs, cybersecurity, AI. This reflects the maturing regulatory architecture of India's financial markets. The few redistributive measures (tax reforms, priority sector lending) carry outsized political weight.

### Agriculture — Almost Entirely Distributive
Every agricultural policy action in 2024-25 — MSP, fertiliser subsidies, oilseed missions, natural farming, warehousing guarantees — is distributive. This reflects a political economy where agricultural constituencies expect tangible, targeted transfers. The absence of regulatory or constituent measures is notable: agricultural regulation (like land reform or contract farming rules) remains a state subject and politically contested.

### Commerce & Industry — Mixed Portfolio
A mix of distributive incentives (electronics manufacturing, space startups, diamond imports) and regulatory adjustments (MSME thresholds, boilers, oilfields). The pattern: incentivise new industries via distribution, regulate existing ones.

### Shipping & Aviation — Regulatory Consolidation
Both sectors saw major legislative overhauls replacing colonial-era statutes. The Bharatiya Vayuyan Vidheyak (aviation), Merchant Shipping Bill, Coastal Shipping Bill, and Bills of Lading Bill all modernise regulatory frameworks. The Indian Ports Bill adds a constituent dimension by creating new governance bodies.

### Law & Justice — Constituent Domain
Supreme Court rulings (mineral taxation, PG medical reservations, arbitrator appointment) and constitutional amendment bills (simultaneous elections) are overwhelmingly constituent. This sector is where the *rules of the game* get rewritten.

### Education — Regulatory Evolution
UGC regulations on faculty/VC appointment, RTE amendments, and PG curriculum frameworks all fall under regulatory. The sector shows a shift toward output-based regulation (competency testing, credit flexibility) rather than input-based controls.

## Cross-Cutting Themes

### 1. Digital Governance Cuts Across Categories
- **Regulatory:** SEBI cybersecurity/AI frameworks, P2P lending rules, real-time data norms
- **Constituent:** Aadhaar authentication expansion, TRAI regulatory sandbox
- **Redistributive:** Income Tax Bill expanding search to "virtual digital spaces"

### 2. Federalism as a Recurring Tension
- SC upholds states' mineral taxation powers (constituent)
- SC strikes down state domicile reservations in PG medical (constituent)
- Simultaneous elections would restructure centre-state electoral dynamics (constituent)
- PMAY, PMGSY, and agricultural missions require centre-state cost sharing (distributive)

### 3. Environmental Policy Straddles Regulatory and Distributive
- End-of-Life Vehicles Rules and Public Liability Insurance amendments are regulatory
- Viability gap funding for offshore wind and green hydrogen incentives are distributive
- This reflects the policy challenge: environmental goals need both carrots and sticks
